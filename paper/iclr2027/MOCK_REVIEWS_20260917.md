## Area chair meta-review

**Summary.** The submission reparameterizes every FFN weight matrix of a pretrained Transformer as softplus(V), initialized at |W_pre|, so that each FFN branch has elementwise nonnegative weights, and fine-tunes. On T5-small (5 seeds, full CNN/DM and XSUM test sets) this costs 2-5% relative ROUGE-L; HotFlip success falls from ~60% to 17% (one seed, n=200). On Pythia-1.4B (3 seeds) constraining both MLP projections lowers HotFlip success from 69.0% to 42.5% at a 5.6x perplexity cost, with random-substitution, query-attack and transfer controls. A conditional gradient-attenuation argument (Sec. 5), a negative order-preservation probe (Sec. 6.3), and an initialization ablation for clean quality (A.10) are included.

**Reviewer agreement and weighting.** Four reviews, all at confidence 4, ratings 5/4/4/4, soundness 2 across the board. There is no substantive disagreement to adjudicate: the reviews converge on the same defects from different angles, and I was able to verify every load-bearing claim against the PDF (see list above: single-seed T5 HotFlip; no gradient-free control on T5; HotFlip weaker than random on the Pythia baseline; the 2.5-2.8x implied clean-NLL ratio on attacked texts vs the stated 1.9x; identical baseline entries across seeds in Table 3; abs-init-free arm matching the monotone model's clean loss with its robustness never measured; nonnegative FFN output under the T5 constraint; CNN/DM being in t5-small's pretraining mixture; the vacuity of the coordinatewise order on mean-centred LayerNorm outputs). The theory reviewer's higher score reflects credit for candor and correct mathematics rather than a different assessment of the evidence; I weight all four equally and find the consensus well-founded.

**Strengths (consensus).** The intervention is simple and precisely specified; the clean-quality evaluation on T5 (five seeds, full test sets, paired tests, effect sizes) is above the usual bar; the Pythia track includes the right anti-gradient-masking controls in kind; the paper reports its own negative results (order preservation, absolute-nats view, truncation bug, non-budget-matched screening) with unusual honesty; the mathematics that is present is correct.

**Why the paper falls short as submitted.** (1) The central causal claim is untested by the authors' own account: A.10 shows an unconstrained model with the same sign-discarding initialization reproduces the monotone model's clean loss exactly, and the robustness of that arm (and the sign-frozen arm) is "left to future work" even though both seed-42 checkpoints exist and a 200-example HotFlip run is trivial. Until this is run, "monotonicity improves robustness" cannot be distinguished from "discarding the pretrained FFN sign pattern produces a worse, less sensitive model." (2) The headline T5 number rests on a single training seed, one weak single-pass HotFlip variant with no true-loss re-scoring, and no gradient-free, random, transfer or loss-matched control on that track; the gradient-free controls exist only on Pythia, where the absolute advantage nearly vanishes (0.77 vs 0.82 nats HotFlip) or reverses (UAT). Sec. 5 itself says such controls are required. (3) The reference attack is demonstrably weak (gradient-guided HotFlip does less damage than random substitution on the Pythia baseline), and the relative success metric, the inclusion of inserted tokens as prediction targets, and the unmatched clean loss all favor the higher-loss constrained model; the T5 UAT experiment is void because the trigger is truncated away for most articles. (4) The framing outruns the evidence: the abstract and Contribution 1 claim order-preserving semantic refinement that Sec. 6.3 refutes; Def. 3.5 (A-monotone with A-dagger) is never instantiated; the coordinatewise order is near-vacuous on the normalized branch input; Sec. 5 is elementary, makes no tested prediction, is inconsistent with the MLP-in result, and yet is cited in 6.3 and A.12 as the explanation; "adaptive", "consistent improvements across two families", and the OOD label for CNN/DM are inaccurate; the seed-coverage sentence in the abstract is misleading. (5) No defense baseline, no alternative constraint, no second model size, no task-level (ROUGE-under-attack) or safety-relevant metric despite the jailbreak motivation.

**Assessment.** The empirical observation (nonnegative-weight FFNs reduce token-substitution sensitivity in two fine-tuned models) is real and honestly reported, and the missing experiments are cheap. But at ICLR's roughly 30% acceptance rate, a paper whose central attribution is unresolved by an experiment the authors already have the checkpoints for, whose headline number is single-seed with no controls, and whose framing is contradicted by its own diagnostic sits clearly below the bar. All four reviewers state they would move to 6 if the ablation-arm robustness result favored the constrained model; that is the right conditional, and it should be resolved before submission rather than in a rebuttal.

## Reviewer 1 (theory / monotone networks)

**Scores:** soundness 2, presentation 3, contribution 2, rating 5, confidence 4

### Summary

The paper proposes imposing elementwise nonnegativity on the weight matrices of Transformer feed-forward (FFN) sublayers (softplus reparameterization, initialized at |W_pre|) so that each FFN branch is a coordinatewise-monotone map of its (normalized) input, while attention, normalization and residuals stay unconstrained. On T5-small the constrained model loses 2-5% relative ROUGE-L (five seeds, full test sets) and HotFlip success drops from ~60% to 17% (n=200, one seed); on Pythia-1.4B constraining both MLP projections cuts HotFlip success from 69% to 42.5% (three seeds) at a 5.6x perplexity cost, with random-substitution and gradient-free query controls showing the advantage is not confined to gradient-based attacks. Section 3 sets up an A-monotone formalism (semantic axes A, right inverse A-dagger, Def. 3.5), Section 5 and Appendix A.4 give a "candidate mechanism" (nonnegative branch Jacobian, saturation-induced attenuation bound, persistence of saturated units), and Section 6.3 reports a probe-based order-preservation diagnostic that comes out negative for the monotone models.

### Strengths

- Unusually candid reporting: the paper states plainly that all models use A = I (p. 3), that the sublayer F is not monotone in h under pre-LN (p. 4, Remark A.1 p. 16), that the mechanism of Section 5 is conditional and that saturation is never measured (p. 6, A.4.3 p. 20), that the order-preservation diagnostic is negative (Section 6.3, p. 8), and that attribution of the robustness gain to constraint vs. initialization is open (A.10, p. 24-25). Reviewers can trust the numbers.
- The Pythia track includes the right anti-gradient-masking controls (random substitutions, gradient-free query attack, cross-model transfer; Table 4, p. 9) and reports absolute nats alongside relative degradation, which correctly exposes that the monotone Pythia model's relative advantage is partly an artifact of its 1.9x higher clean NLL.
- The constraint-scope screening (Table 7, p. 24) and the initialization ablation for clean quality (Table 8, p. 25) are the kind of controls that most architectural-defense papers omit; the MLP-in result (robustness gets worse) is an informative negative.
- The mathematics that is present is correct: I verified Prop. 3.3, Prop. 3.4, Lemma 5.1 (including the Clarke-Jacobian remark), the chain-rule decomposition in A.4 (p. 18), the triangle/Cauchy-Schwarz bound of Lemma 5.3 (A.3.3, p. 17), Lemma A.2, and the GELU constants in Assumption 5.2 (GELU' < 0 for z < -0.752; |GELU'| non-decreasing on (-inf, -sqrt(2)] since GELU'' = phi(z)(2 - z^2)).
- Multi-seed, full-test-set ROUGE evaluation with paired tests and effect sizes (Section 6.1, Table 1, p. 7) is above the usual bar for the clean-quality side of the trade-off.

### Weaknesses

- Definitions do not describe the trained models (Section 3, pp. 3-4; Remark A.1, p. 16). Definition 3.5 defines an A-monotone Transformer by F(h) = h + A^+ g(Ah), and Prop. 3.3 proves A-monotonicity of that F. The models actually trained implement F(h) = h + g(LN(h)), which the paper concedes 'need not be coordinatewise monotone as a map of h'. So no trained model is a 'monotone Transformer' in the sense of Def. 3.5, and Prop. 3.3 is never instantiated. The A / A^+ / preorder / kernel apparatus (p. 3) and the p < d discussion are never used (all runs have A = I). The object the paper studies is simply 'a Transformer whose FFN branches have nonnegative weights'; Section 3 should be rewritten to define that object and drop the unused generality, or an experiment with a learned A should be added.
- The coordinatewise order on the branch input is (near-)vacuous under normalization (Section 3 p. 4; Remark A.1 p. 16). For GPT-NeoX the branch input is u = gamma * n + beta with n mean-centred; if the gain vector gamma is sign-uniform, u <= u' elementwise with both mean-centred forces u = u', so no two distinct realizable branch inputs are comparable and 'monotone in u' carries no order-theoretic content on the domain g actually sees. For T5's RMSNorm the comparable pairs form a codimension-one set. The paper should either state this or measure how often ordered prompt pairs produce comparable branch inputs; as written, the semantic-order motivation of the Introduction does not connect to the constraint at the point where it is applied.
- The numbered theory is textbook and occupies ~3.5 of 9 main-text pages. Lemma 3.2 (composition of monotone maps), Prop. 3.4 (nonnegative weights + non-decreasing activation => monotone MLP; Archer-Wang 1993 / Sill 1998 / Daniels-Velikova 2010), Lemma 5.1 (product of nonnegative matrices is nonnegative), Lemma 5.3 (triangle + Cauchy-Schwarz on rank-one terms) and Lemma A.2 (one line from W_1 >= 0) are all immediate. Lemma 5.3 explicitly does not use nonnegativity ('Nonnegativity is not needed for the bound', p. 6), so it applies verbatim to the unconstrained baseline; for ReLU the epsilon = 0 case is just the definition of the ReLU Jacobian, and the 'in particular' limit clause adds nothing for ReLU. Lemmas 5.1 and 5.3 concern the fictitious map T(s) = s + g(s), which under pre-LN is not a map in the network (the paper says so, p. 5 and p. 18). Main-text space should go to experiments; the facts can be stated in two sentences with citations.
- The 'mechanism' (Section 5, pp. 5-6; A.4, pp. 17-20) is decorative with respect to the empirical robustness. (a) It predicts attenuation of the FFN's *own* gradient contribution only under sign-coherent downstream gradients (all coordinates of grad_{s'}L of one sign, p. 6 and p. 19), which essentially never holds for a 512- or 2048-dimensional hidden-state gradient; under mixed signs the paper admits 'removing a unit's contribution can increase the norm'. (b) Even in the sign-coherent case, J_T >= I elementwise means the constrained branch can never reduce the gradient norm below the residual-path norm, whereas an unconstrained branch can cancel; so the nonnegativity part of the argument points toward *larger*, not smaller, gradients relative to the baseline. (c) Nothing in Section 5 is measured: no saturation fractions, no gradient norms w.r.t. token embeddings, no comparison of HotFlip's first-order estimate vs. realized loss change for monotone vs. baseline. (d) The Pythia data contradict the mechanism's discriminating power: Lemma 5.3 and Lemma A.2 both hold for the MLP-in scope (only W_1 >= 0 is needed), yet MLP-in makes HotFlip success rise from 71.5% to 83.5% (Table 7, p. 24); and on the baseline HotFlip is *weaker* than random substitution (55.2% vs 64.6%, Table 4, p. 9), so gradient guidance is not what the monotone model is defending against. (e) The gradient-free controls show the monotone Pythia model is uniformly less sensitive to every substitution type, which a gradient-attenuation story does not address. Section 6.3 (p. 8) nonetheless attributes the gains 'to the local sensitivity properties of the constrained sublayers (Section 5)' and A.12 (p. 25) says the analysis 'offers insight into why these improvements arise'; those sentences overreach what Section 5 establishes.
- The central causal claim (monotonicity, not initialization, produces robustness) is untested by the authors' own account (Intro p. 2; Discussion p. 9; A.10 pp. 24-25). Table 8 shows the unconstrained 'abs-init-free' arm reaches exactly the monotone model's validation loss (2.51 vs 2.51), i.e., the entire clean-quality cost is the destroyed sign pattern. If that arm is also HotFlip-robust, the paper's thesis collapses to 'discarding pretrained FFN signs makes T5-small robust'. Both ablation checkpoints already exist (seed 42); the HotFlip evaluation is n = 200 examples. This is the single most important missing result and is cheap.
- The headline T5 robustness number (60% -> 17%, abstract) rests on one gradient-based attack with no gradient-free control on that track. Section 5 itself says gradient-free controls 'are needed to establish that the observed robustness is not an artifact of gradient-based optimization' (p. 6), but they were only run on Pythia (Section 6.4). UATs are ineffective against every T5 model (< 1% NLL, p. 7) and are truncated away for long articles (A.8, p. 21). Table 2 is a single training seed (2024) on n = 200 with a seed-42 corroboration on n = 100. Random-substitution and query-attack controls on the five T5 seeds are needed before the abstract can claim an attack-agnostic robustness gain in the encoder-decoder setting.
- Abstract and Contribution 1 (pp. 1-2) still claim 'monotone language models whose semantic refinement is order-preserving' and 'monotone feed-forward sublayers that enforce order-preserving semantic refinement', but Section 6.3 / Figure 1 (p. 8) show the monotone T5 encoder is *less* order-preserving along learned directions at layers 3-5 (0.42-0.45 vs 0.78-0.92) and monotone Pythia is worse at every depth. The abstract should be brought into line with Section 6.3 and Section 7.
- A structural consequence that is stronger and more informative than 'monotone' is never stated. In the T5 track g(u) = W_o ReLU(W_i u) with no biases (confirmed in T5DenseReluDense), so the FFN output is elementwise nonnegative for every input: the FFN branch can only add vectors in the nonnegative orthant to the residual stream, and with ReLU each output coordinate is convex and positively homogeneous in u. In Pythia, W_out GELU(.) + b_out is bounded below by a fixed vector, so the branch output lives in a translated orthant. This confines the FFN's contribution to one of 2^d orthants, is plausibly the real source of both the quality cost and the reduced sensitivity, and is directly testable by an 'MLP-out only' scope (W_out >= 0, W_in free), which is absent from Table 7 while the uninformative MLP-in scope is included. Relatedly, because the RMSNorm gain gamma is unconstrained, the effective first-layer map on the normalized state is W_i diag(gamma), so the branch is monotone w.r.t. the order induced by diag(sign gamma), not the coordinatewise order; the paper's 'A = I' statement (p. 3) is therefore not literally what the trained network implements.
- Universal-approximation remark (p. 3-4) is imprecise: Daniels-Velikova 2010 requires multiple hidden layers for nonnegative-weight sigmoidal networks; the one-hidden-layer FFN considered here is not covered even for sigmoid, and the paper should say so rather than only noting the ReLU convexity restriction.

### Questions

- Please run HotFlip (and, ideally, the random-substitution control) on the seed-42 'abs-init-free' and 'sign-frozen' checkpoints of Table 8. Is the unconstrained model initialized at |W_pre| also robust? This determines whether the paper's title claim is supported.
- For the trained Pythia-1.4B models, what fraction of LayerNorm gain entries feeding the MLP are negative? If gains are sign-uniform, do you agree that no two distinct realizable branch inputs are comparable in the coordinatewise order, and how does that affect the semantic-order interpretation of the constraint?
- Can you report, for monotone vs. baseline T5 (any seed), (i) the fraction of inactive ReLU units per FFN layer on clean inputs and (ii) the norm of the loss gradient w.r.t. token embeddings? Without these, is there any empirical evidence that the saturation/attenuation mechanism of Section 5 is operative?
- How does Section 5 account for the MLP-in result (Table 7)? Lemma 5.3 and Lemma A.2 hold for that scope (they need only W_1 >= 0), yet HotFlip success rises by 12 points. Which ingredient of the mechanism is supposed to fail there?
- Would you consider replacing the A-monotone formalism of Section 3 with a direct definition of the object trained (nonnegative-weight FFN branches under pre-LN), and moving Lemma 3.2 / Prop. 3.4 / Lemma 5.1 / Lemma 5.3 to a short appendix paragraph with citations?
- Have you tried an 'MLP-out only' scope on Pythia? Given that the branch output is confined to a (translated) nonnegative orthant only when W_out >= 0, this is the natural test of whether the orthant restriction, rather than monotonicity per se, drives the effect.
- Why are no gradient-free controls reported on the T5 track, given that Section 5 says they are required and the headline robustness number is from T5?

### Rating justification

The empirical core is a genuine and honestly reported finding: nonnegative FFN weights in fine-tuned T5-small and Pythia-1.4B reduce token-substitution vulnerability, and the Pythia controls are the right ones. But, judged as submitted, the paper does not establish its own thesis. The theoretical sections (3, 5, A.2-A.4) contain only immediate facts, the formal object defined (Def. 3.5) is not the object trained, the coordinatewise order is (near-)trivial on the normalized branch input, and the 'mechanism' is neither measured nor consistent with the MLP-in and random-substitution results; it functions as decoration around an empirical result. More importantly, the authors' own ablation shows the initialization alone reproduces the clean-quality effect, and the corresponding robustness evaluation, which would decide whether monotonicity or sign destruction is responsible, is left to future work despite requiring only an attack run on existing checkpoints. The headline T5 number also lacks a gradient-free control. The abstract overclaims order preservation that Section 6.3 refutes. These are fixable, which is why this is a 5 rather than a 3, but in its current form the contribution is a promising empirical observation with an unresolved confound and a theoretical framing that does not do explanatory work.

### What would raise the score

- Report HotFlip and random-substitution results for the 'abs-init-free' and 'sign-frozen' T5 arms of Table 8; if the constrained model is clearly more robust than abs-init-free, the causal claim is supported and I would move to 6.
- Add random-substitution and gradient-free query controls on the T5 track (all five seeds), so the headline encoder-decoder claim does not rest on a single gradient-based attack at one seed.
- Replace Section 3's unused A-monotone formalism with a definition of the trained object (nonnegative-weight FFN branches under pre-LN), state the orthant-confinement consequence explicitly, and cut the textbook lemmas to a paragraph; use the freed space for experiments.
- Either test the Section 5 mechanism (saturation fractions, embedding-gradient norms, first-order vs. realized loss change for monotone vs. baseline) or demote it to a short remark and remove the sentences in 6.3 and A.12 that attribute the gains to it.
- Add an 'MLP-out only' scope to the Pythia screening to separate orthant confinement of the FFN output from full-branch monotonicity.
- Bring the abstract and Contribution 1 into agreement with Section 6.3: do not claim order-preserving semantic refinement.

## Reviewer 2 (adversarial robustness in NLP)

**Scores:** soundness 2, presentation 3, contribution 2, rating 4, confidence 4

### Summary

The paper proposes constraining the feed-forward sublayers of a pretrained Transformer to be monotone by reparameterizing every FFN weight matrix as softplus(V) (elementwise nonnegative), initialized at |W_pre|, and fine-tuning. On T5-small (fine-tuned on DialogSum/HighlightSum/arXiv, evaluated OOD on CNN/DM and XSUM over five seeds) the monotone model loses 2-5% relative ROUGE-L and, on one seed and 200 examples, reduces HotFlip success from 59.5% (fine-tuned baseline) to 17.0% (Table 2). Universal triggers are ineffective against all T5 models (<1% NLL increase), partly because the prepended trigger is truncated away for most CNN/DM articles. On Pythia-1.4B, constraining both MLP projections lowers HotFlip success from 69.0% to 42.5% (three seeds) at a 5.6x perplexity cost; random-substitution, gradient-free query, and transfer controls (Table 4) show the monotone model is less sensitive in relative terms, but absolute NLL gaps are small (HotFlip 0.77 vs 0.82 nats) and reversed for triggers (0.40 vs 0.32 nats). A conditional gradient-attenuation analysis (Section 5) and a negative order-preservation probe result (Section 6.3) are included; an ablation (A.10) attributes the clean-quality cost to the loss of the pretrained sign pattern, but the analogous robustness attribution is left to future work.

### Strengths

- Simple, well-specified method (Section 3, p. 3-4): softplus reparameterization with magnitude-preserving initialization from the pretrained checkpoint, applied to every FFN matrix; the constrained scope is stated exactly and the GELU/pre-norm caveats are spelled out (Section 4, Remark A.1).
- Clean-quality evaluation on T5 is thorough: five training seeds, full CNN/DM and XSUM test sets, per-example paired tests, effect sizes, and a well-designed attribution ablation (A.10, Table 8) that isolates the initialization cost from the constraint cost.
- The authors report absolute loss increases alongside relative degradation (Tables 2, 3, 4) and explicitly flag that part of the Pythia relative advantage is an artifact of higher clean loss (Section 6.4, p. 9; A.9, p. 24). They also report the negative order-preservation result (Section 6.3, Figure 1) rather than hiding it.
- The Pythia track includes the right kinds of gradient-masking controls in principle: a random-substitution baseline, a gradient-free query attack, and a full crafted-on x evaluated-on transfer matrix (Table 4, Table 9), citing Athalye et al. / Carlini et al. / Tramer et al.
- The theory (Section 5, A.3, A.4) is modest and honestly scoped as conditional; the proofs I checked (Lemma 5.1, Lemma 5.3, Lemma A.2) are correct, and the text is explicit that the analysis covers only one FFN branch and that saturation is not shown to occur.
- Writing is clear and the related work on monotone networks and on obfuscated gradients is appropriate.

### Weaknesses

- The headline T5 robustness claim (abstract, p. 1; Table 2, p. 7) rests on a single training seed (2024), 200 examples, and one weak, non-iterative HotFlip variant (A.8, p. 21: top-5 positions by gradient norm, replacement by gradient-embedding dot product, no re-scoring of candidates by true loss, no beam search, no iteration), with no random-substitution, query-based, or transfer control on T5 at all. The gradient-free controls exist only on Pythia. The abstract's 'arguing against gradient masking' therefore does not cover the 60%->17% number that the paper is built around. The five T5 checkpoints already exist and T5-small HotFlip on 200 examples is cheap: run HotFlip on all five seeds and add the random and query controls to the T5 track.
- HotFlip is weaker than random substitution on the Pythia baseline (Table 4, p. 9: 55.2% vs 64.6% degradation; acknowledged in A.9, p. 24). A gradient-guided attack that underperforms random replacement indicates a weak attack implementation (first-order argmax without true-loss candidate re-scoring), not a model property, and it casts doubt on every HotFlip comparison in Tables 2, 3 and 7 because the T5 implementation follows 'the same attack logic' (A.8, p. 22). Implement HotFlip with top-k true-loss re-scoring and/or beam search (as in Ebrahimi et al. 2018 and Wallace et al. 2019), or GCG, and report degradation as a function of flip budget (1..k flips) for both models.
- The success-rate metric (degradation above 10%/15% of each model's own clean loss; Section 4, p. 4) mechanically favors the model with higher clean loss. On Pythia the monotone clean NLL is ~1.9x higher (Table 3), and in absolute nats the HotFlip gap essentially disappears (0.77 vs 0.82, Table 4) while triggers are worse for the monotone model (0.40 vs 0.32 nats, Table 3). Moreover, because the substituted/trigger tokens are themselves prediction targets (A.8, p. 23), a model with a flatter predictive distribution assigns higher likelihood to inserted junk tokens and will show lower relative sensitivity to random substitutions for that reason alone, so the random-substitution control is confounded by the clean-quality gap rather than being a clean refutation of gradient masking. Actionable: (a) mask the substituted and trigger positions out of the loss and re-report; (b) define success on an absolute-nats threshold or show success-vs-threshold curves; (c) compare against a clean-loss-matched unconstrained model.
- The constraint-versus-initialization confound for robustness is left open (Introduction p. 2; Discussion p. 9; A.10 p. 24-25) even though the sign-frozen and abs-init-free T5 models have already been trained at seed 42 and the abs-init-free arm reaches exactly the monotone model's validation loss (Table 8), making it the ideal clean-loss-matched control. Running Table 2's HotFlip on these two checkpoints costs minutes. Without it the paper cannot claim that monotonicity, rather than discarding the pretrained sign pattern and retraining a worse model, is responsible for the robustness gain.
- The T5 universal-trigger experiment (Section 4, p. 4; A.8, p. 21; Section 6.2, p. 7) is uninformative rather than a 'lower bound': the trigger is prepended and then left-truncated away for every article longer than ~505 tokens, which is the majority of CNN/DM, so on most examples the attacked input is identical to the clean input and sub-1% NLL increases follow trivially. A lower bound on attack effectiveness cannot support a robustness claim (robustness needs strong attacks). Insert the trigger after truncation (or append it), report the fraction of evaluation articles on which the trigger survived, and re-run; otherwise remove universal triggers from Contribution 3 (p. 2).
- Terminology and framing overreach. Section 6.2 (p. 7) calls the attack 'the adaptive HotFlip attack', but nothing is adapted to the defense in the sense of Tramer et al. (2020); the abstract and introduction (p. 1) motivate the work with jailbreaks and safety constraints, yet no experiment involves instructions, safety behavior, or any change in generated output under HotFlip (ROUGE under HotFlip is never reported; only the negligible UAT ROUGE changes in A.11). Either report generation-level effects (ROUGE under HotFlip, or a targeted Seq2Sick-style attack) or narrow the claims to teacher-forced loss sensitivity and drop 'adaptive' and the jailbreak framing.
- The gradient-free controls on Pythia are weak by the standards of the query-attack literature (A.8, p. 22): candidates come from a fixed pool of only 100 token ids, the query attack tries 20 candidates at 10 random positions on 50 texts (202 forward passes), and positions are not chosen by importance. Strengthen with a larger candidate set (full vocabulary or embedding-nearest-neighbor sets), importance-based position selection (as in TextFooler/BERT-Attack), more texts, and a query-budget vs degradation curve.
- The abstract's clause 'consistent across five training seeds on full test sets' is attached to the sentence containing the attack numbers and can easily be read as applying to them; the attack numbers are single-seed (T5) and the seed-42 corroboration in A.9 (p. 23) is an 'earlier evaluation' on 100 examples whose protocol equivalence is not stated. Rewrite the abstract so the seed coverage of each number is unambiguous, and state whether the seed-42 evaluation used the identical protocol.
- Minor: the success thresholds differ between tracks (10% vs 15%) with no justification and no sensitivity analysis; the HotFlip budget (5 flips of up to 512 tokens) is never swept; the abstract's 'without ... modified training objectives' sits uneasily with the monotone models receiving 2x epochs, 5x learning rate and longer warmup on Pythia (A.7); and the conclusion's 'consistent improvements across two Transformer families' (p. 9) is not supported by the absolute Pythia numbers.

### Questions

- Table 4: dividing the absolute delta-NLL by the relative degradation implies clean NLLs of ~1.4 nats (baseline) and ~3.7 nats (monotone) on the attacked texts, a ratio of 2.5-2.8x, not the 'roughly 1.9x' stated on p. 9 (which matches ln(37.68)/ln(6.72) on the held-out perplexity set). Are degradations means of per-example ratios? What is the actual clean-NLL ratio on the 200 attack texts, and does the absolute-vs-relative caveat get correspondingly stronger?
- In the T5 HotFlip (A.8), are the top-k first-order candidates ever re-scored by the true loss, and is the attack single-pass (all five flips chosen from one gradient) or iterative? Same question for the Pythia 'unrestricted argmax'.
- What fraction of the 1,500 T5 trigger-evaluation articles exceed ~505 tokens (i.e., on what fraction did the trigger survive truncation)? Is the clean input also left-truncated, or right-truncated as in the default tokenizer setting?
- Did the seed-42 HotFlip evaluation (A.9, p. 23) use exactly the protocol of Table 2 (same positions/candidate rule, same 10% threshold)? If so, why not report all five seeds?
- If attack success is redefined on an absolute threshold (e.g. delta-NLL >= 0.3 nats for both models), what are the T5 and Pythia success rates?
- What are the HotFlip results for the sign-frozen and abs-init-free arms of A.10 at seed 42?
- Does the monotone T5 model's ROUGE-L drop more or less than the baseline's under the HotFlip perturbations of Table 2?
- For the Pythia loss convention: if the substituted and trigger positions are masked out of the NLL, do the random-substitution and query-attack gaps in Table 4 survive in absolute terms?

### Rating justification

The idea is simple and the paper is unusually candid about its caveats, but judged against the standards the adversarial-robustness community applies to a defense paper (Carlini et al. 2019 checklist), the central evidence is thin. The headline 60%->17% T5 result is a single seed, 200 examples, one weak single-pass HotFlip variant, and has no gradient-free, random, transfer, or clean-loss-matched control on that track; the authors' own Pythia numbers show this HotFlip implementation is weaker than random substitution, which undermines it as a reference attack. On the Pythia track, where controls do exist, the absolute-nats advantage is marginal (0.77 vs 0.82 HotFlip) or reversed (triggers), the relative advantage is confounded by a 5.6x perplexity gap and by counting inserted tokens as prediction targets, and the query/random attacks use a 100-token candidate pool. The T5 UAT experiment is effectively void because the trigger is truncated away on most articles. Most importantly, the constraint-versus-initialization confound for robustness is left open although the matched-clean-loss control model (abs-init-free) already exists and evaluating it is trivial. The paper's framing (jailbreaks, 'adaptive' attack, 'consistent improvements across two families') outruns what is shown. The missing experiments are all cheap, so this is fixable, but as submitted the robustness claim in the abstract is not supported at the strength stated.

### What would raise the score

- Run HotFlip (and the random-substitution, query, and transfer controls) on all five T5 seeds, and evaluate the sign-frozen and abs-init-free A.10 arms under the same attacks; if the monotone model still beats the clean-loss-matched abs-init-free arm in absolute nats, the causal claim is established and this would move me to 6.
- Strengthen the attacks: HotFlip with true-loss re-scoring of top-k candidates and/or beam search, a GCG-style attack, and degradation-vs-budget curves for both models on both tracks; the reference gradient attack must at least beat random substitution on the baseline.
- Fix the metric confounds: mask substituted/trigger positions out of the Pythia loss, report success on an absolute-nats threshold (or success-vs-threshold curves), and compare against a clean-loss-matched unconstrained baseline.
- Re-run the T5 universal-trigger attack with the trigger inserted after truncation and report the fraction of examples on which it is present.
- Report a generation-level effect of the attack (ROUGE or a targeted Seq2Sick-style attack) or narrow the abstract/introduction to teacher-forced loss sensitivity; remove 'adaptive', the jailbreak framing, and 'consistent improvements across two Transformer families' unless the absolute Pythia numbers support it.

## Reviewer 3 (Transformer architectures / LLM training)

**Scores:** soundness 2, presentation 3, contribution 2, rating 4, confidence 4

### Summary

The paper proposes "selective monotonicity" for Transformer language models: all linear maps inside every feed-forward sublayer are reparameterized as softplus(V) so they are elementwise nonnegative (with the model's own ReLU/GELU), while attention, residuals and normalization are unconstrained. Pretrained weights are re-initialized at |W_pre|+eps (the sign pattern is discarded) and the model is then fine-tuned. On T5-small (fine-tuned on DialogSum/HighlightSum/arXiv, evaluated on CNN/DM and XSUM, five seeds) the constrained model loses 2-5% relative ROUGE-L (10% ROUGE-2; validation perplexity 1.35x) while the HotFlip success rate drops from 59.5% (fine-tuned baseline) to 17.0% (single seed, n=200). On Pythia-1.4B (recovery-trained on the Pile) constraining both MLP projections lowers HotFlip success from 69.0% to 42.5% (three seeds) at a 5.6x held-out perplexity cost; a random-substitution control and a gradient-free query attack also show smaller relative degradation for the constrained model. Section 5 gives a conditional local gradient-attenuation argument (nonnegative Jacobian plus saturation), an order-preservation probe (Sec. 6.3) returns a negative result (the constrained models are not more order-preserving end-to-end), and Appendix A.10 shows that the entire clean-quality cost on T5 is explained by the |W_pre| initialization rather than by the constraint, while leaving the corresponding robustness attribution untested.

### Strengths

- Simple, clearly specified intervention (Sec. 3, p. 4): a softplus reparameterization of FFN weights with a stated initialization, applied to two public checkpoints; easy to reproduce and the protocols in App. A.5-A.8 are complete (hyperparameters, seeds, precision per stage, candidate vocabularies).
- Honest reporting of negative and confounding results: the order-preservation diagnostic (Sec. 6.3, Fig. 1, p. 8) contradicts the paper's own motivation and is reported as such; absolute (nats) degradation is reported alongside relative degradation (Tables 2 and 4) and the authors explicitly note that part of the Pythia advantage reflects higher clean loss (p. 9); the truncation bug in the T5 trigger protocol is disclosed (Sec. 4, App. A.8).
- Gradient-masking controls on the Pythia track (Table 4, p. 9): random substitutions, a query-based gradient-free attack, and a crafted-on x evaluated-on transfer matrix, following Athalye/Carlini/Tramer guidance; the asymmetric transfer pattern is a genuinely informative observation.
- Appendix A.10 (p. 24-25) is a well-designed two-arm ablation (sign-frozen softplus vs. unconstrained abs-init) that cleanly isolates the source of the clean-quality gap; this is the kind of control most architecture-constraint papers omit.
- Multi-seed T5 quality evaluation on full test sets (n=11,490 and 11,333) with per-example paired tests and effect sizes (Sec. 6.1, p. 7), and three-seed validation of the selected Pythia scope (Table 3, p. 8).

### Weaknesses

- The central causal claim is not established by the paper's own ablation. App. A.10 (p. 24-25) shows the abs-init-free arm (unconstrained, initialized at |W_pre|) reaches exactly the same validation loss as the monotone model (2.51 vs 2.51, Table 8), i.e. the 'monotone' model is behaviorally indistinguishable in clean loss from a plain unconstrained model whose FFN signs were scrambled. The robustness of the sign-frozen and abs-init-free arms is never measured, although both seed-42 checkpoints exist and a 200-example HotFlip run costs minutes. The abstract (p. 1) and Contribution 3 (p. 2) nevertheless attribute the robustness gain to 'this simple structural constraint'. Until HotFlip/query results are reported for these two arms (and ideally a loss-matched unconstrained control such as an early-stopped baseline or a smaller model), the paper cannot distinguish 'monotonicity improves robustness' from 'a degraded, re-learned FFN is less sensitive to everything'.
- The clean-performance cost on the decoder-only track is prohibitive for any practitioner, and the paper does not confront this quantitatively. Held-out Pile perplexity goes from 6.72 to 37.68 (5.61x; Table 3, p. 8; range 5.21-6.25x, App. A.12), which is far worse than much smaller unconstrained models of the same family. Under the strongest attack the paper ran (query attack, Table 4, p. 9) the absolute degradation is 1.17 vs 1.39 nats, a 16% reduction, purchased with a 5.6x perplexity increase. No perplexity-matched unconstrained comparison (e.g. Pythia-160M/410M under the same attacks) is provided, so the reader cannot tell whether the constrained 1.4B model sits above or below the trivial 'use a worse model' Pareto frontier.
- The attack suite is weak, which inflates the headline numbers. On Pythia, the paper's own Table 4 (p. 9) shows gradient-guided HotFlip (55.2% degradation on the baseline) is less damaging than uniformly random substitutions (64.6%), and App. A.9 (p. 24) concedes that 'the first-order approximation underlying HotFlip is a weak guide for this model'; the headline 69.0% -> 42.5% SR (Table 3) is therefore measured with an attack weaker than random noise. On T5, HotFlip picks replacements purely by gradient-embedding dot product with no forward-pass re-scoring or beam (App. A.8, p. 21), and no gradient-free or query attack is run on T5 at all, so the 60% -> 17% T5 result rests on one weak gradient attack. GCG (Zou et al., 2023) and GBDA (Guo et al., 2021) are cited in Related Work (p. 2) but not used.
- The T5 HotFlip result, the paper's cleanest number, is single-seed: Table 2 (p. 7) uses the seed-2024 checkpoints with n=200, 'corroborated' by an earlier n=100 evaluation of seed 42 under a possibly different protocol (App. A.6, p. 20, App. A.9, p. 23). Five seeds of every model exist; running the same n=200 attack on all of them and reporting mean +/- s.d. is cheap and would match the rigor of the ROUGE evaluation.
- Constraint-scope screening (Table 7, p. 24; Sec. 6.4, p. 8) is single-seed and not budget-matched: MLP-in was trained at the full budget while MLP-both and MLP-in+Attn-out used a 30k-sample reduced budget with a different baseline (6.67 vs 7.06 PPL), and MLP-in+Attn-out reaches 88x perplexity. Yet Contribution 4 (p. 2) and the abstract state as a finding that 'constraining the feed-forward input projection alone is ineffective'. The MLP-in SR increase (71.5% -> 83.5%) on one seed with a 3.65x perplexity model is not sufficient evidence for a headline claim about where constraints must be placed.
- Robustness is measured only as loss/NLL increase, not as an effect on the behaviors that motivate the paper. The introduction (p. 1-2) motivates the work with jailbreaks and safety, but no jailbreak, refusal, classification, or generation-quality-under-attack metric is reported; ROUGE under HotFlip is never given for T5 (only under the near-null UAT, App. A.11), so a 0.14-nat loss increase (Table 2) cannot be translated into whether summaries actually get worse. The success-rate thresholds (10% and 15% relative loss increase, Sec. 4) are arbitrary and, being relative, favor the higher-clean-loss constrained model.
- The 'out-of-distribution' framing of the T5 evaluation (Sec. 4, p. 4; App. A.5, p. 20: 'Neither CNN/DailyMail nor XSUM is used in training') is inaccurate for the checkpoint used: the original t5-small (the ReLU variant the paper relies on) was pretrained on a supervised multi-task mixture that includes CNN/DailyMail summarization with the same 'summarize:' prefix, which is why the un-fine-tuned 'standard' model already scores ROUGE-1 38.6 (Table 1). Relatedly, fine-tuning on DialogSum/HighlightSum/arXiv does not improve CNN/DM ROUGE-L over the pretrained model (33.2 vs 33.2) and lowers XSUM ROUGE-L (13.1 vs 13.5, Table 6, p. 23), and the in-distribution ROUGE on the fine-tuning corpora is never reported, so the 'modest' 2-5% cost is measured on a setup where the baseline itself is weak; the 0.30-nat validation-loss gap (Table 5) suggests the in-distribution cost is larger.
- The mechanism of Section 5 (p. 5-6) is explicitly conditional on saturation, and the paper states it 'does not test' whether training under nonnegativity produces saturation (p. 6; App. A.4.3, p. 19). Measuring the fraction of inactive ReLU units per FFN layer in the monotone vs baseline T5 models on the attack examples is a one-line diagnostic that would either support or falsify the proposed mechanism; its absence leaves Section 5 as untested theory occupying a page of the main text.
- Framing versus evidence: the title, abstract, and Section 1 sell 'monotonicity' and semantic order preservation, but Sec. 6.3 (p. 8) shows the constrained T5 encoder is markedly less order-preserving at layers 3-5 (0.42-0.45 vs 0.78-0.92) and the constrained Pythia model is less order-preserving at every depth; the paper's own conclusion (p. 9) is that the semantic-order motivation is 'the design principle' but not a property of the trained models. What is actually demonstrated is that nonnegative-weight FFNs (whose output g(u) = W_o ReLU(W_i u) is itself elementwise nonnegative in T5, so every FFN can only add non-negative values to every residual coordinate, a much stronger restriction than 'monotone' that the paper never discusses) reduce sensitivity to token substitutions. The paper would be more credible if framed around that.
- No comparison to any existing defense. The paper offers no adversarial-training, Lipschitz-regularization (Newhouse et al., 2025, cited p. 1), perplexity-filtering, or smoothing baseline at matched clean cost, so a practitioner has no way to judge whether 5% ROUGE-L (T5) or 5.6x perplexity (Pythia) for the observed HotFlip reduction is competitive with, e.g., a few epochs of HotFlip-augmented fine-tuning of the unconstrained baseline.
- Scale: T5-small (60M) and Pythia-1.4B only (acknowledged p. 9, App. A.12). Howe et al. (2025), cited on p. 1, show robustness trends change with scale; with no second size in either family the paper provides no evidence the effect or the cost persists, which limits the practical relevance for the 'large language models' of the title.

### Questions

- In Table 3 (p. 8) the baseline perplexity (6.72) and HotFlip success rate (69.0%) are identical across the three seeds to the reported precision, while the monotone runs vary widely (35.0-42.0 PPL). Was the baseline recovery-trained separately per seed as App. A.7 states, or was one baseline checkpoint reused? If separate, why is there no variance?
- Can you report HotFlip (and the query attack, for Pythia) on the sign-frozen and abs-init-free arms of App. A.10? Since abs-init-free matches the monotone model's clean loss exactly, this single experiment determines whether the paper's main claim holds.
- With W_o >= 0, ReLU, and no bias in T5, the FFN output g(u) is elementwise nonnegative, so every FFN sublayer can only increase every coordinate of the residual stream; only attention (and the final LN gain) can decrease coordinates. Did you observe the residual-stream norm or mean drifting with depth, and could this explain the intermediate-layer collapse of the order-preservation probe in Fig. 1?
- Why were CNN/DM and XSUM chosen as 'out-of-distribution' targets for a checkpoint whose pretraining mixture contains CNN/DM, and what is the ROUGE on the DialogSum/HighlightSum/arXiv test splits for baseline vs monotone?
- For the T5 HotFlip attack, are candidate replacements verified by a forward pass, or is the first-order dot-product argmax accepted directly? If the latter, how does the success rate change with a beam or with re-scoring, and does a query-based attack on T5 preserve the 60% -> 17% gap?
- What is the fraction of saturated (inactive) ReLU units per FFN layer in the trained monotone vs baseline T5 models on the attack set? This directly tests the Section 5 mechanism.
- Given that the MLP-in scope was screened at the full budget and the other scopes at a reduced budget (Table 7), can the MLP-both and MLP-in+Attn-out screening be repeated at the full budget on at least the three seeds of Table 3, and does the 'MLP-in alone is ineffective' claim survive?
- Does ROUGE-L actually decrease under HotFlip for the T5 models, and by how much for baseline vs monotone? A 0.14-nat loss increase may or may not change the generated summary.

### Rating justification

The paper is carefully written and unusually honest about its caveats, and the intervention is simple and reproducible. But the evidence does not support the claim the paper makes. Appendix A.10 shows that an unconstrained model with the same |W_pre| initialization matches the monotone model's clean loss exactly, and the robustness of that arm is never measured, so the paper cannot attribute the robustness gain to the constraint rather than to a disrupted-and-relearned FFN; the authors themselves flag this as open (p. 2, p. 9). The attacks are weak (HotFlip on Pythia is less damaging than random substitutions; T5 uses a no-rescoring HotFlip and no gradient-free attack), the T5 HotFlip headline is single-seed, the scope-screening that underwrites Contribution 4 is single-seed and not budget-matched, and the 'out-of-distribution' T5 evaluation is mislabeled. From a practitioner's standpoint the trade-off is unattractive: a 5.6x perplexity increase on Pythia-1.4B buys a 16% reduction in absolute degradation under the strongest attack, no defense baseline is compared, and there is no evidence at any second model size. The order-preservation diagnostic is negative, which undermines the paper's own framing. These are fixable problems, and the required experiments (attacking the A.10 arms, multi-seed HotFlip, a loss-matched control, a budget-matched screen) are cheap, but as submitted the paper is below the acceptance threshold.

### What would raise the score

- Report HotFlip and query-attack results for the sign-frozen and abs-init-free arms of App. A.10 (and for a clean-loss-matched unconstrained control, e.g. an early-stopped baseline or a smaller model of the same family). If the constrained model is clearly more robust than the abs-init-free arm at equal clean loss, the main claim is established and the score would rise to 6.
- Strengthen and complete the attack evaluation: run HotFlip on all five T5 seeds (n=200 each) with forward-pass re-scoring; add a gradient-free query attack on T5; add at least one modern optimizer-based attack (GCG or GBDA) on Pythia; report ROUGE-L under HotFlip for T5 and, ideally, a downstream task metric rather than only loss increase.
- Repeat the Pythia constraint-scope screening (Table 7) at the full budget on three seeds so that Contribution 4 rests on budget-matched, multi-seed evidence.
- Add at least one existing defense baseline (HotFlip-augmented adversarial fine-tuning, Lipschitz regularization, or perplexity filtering) at matched clean cost, and plot the clean-quality vs robustness Pareto frontier including a smaller unconstrained model, so that the practical value of the trade-off is visible.
- Correct the OOD claim for CNN/DM, report in-distribution ROUGE on the fine-tuning corpora, and fix the T5 trigger truncation (insert the trigger after left-truncation) so the UAT numbers are meaningful.
- Empirically test the Section 5 mechanism by measuring per-layer saturation fractions in monotone vs baseline FFNs, and discuss the nonnegative-output property of the T5 FFN under the constraint.
- Provide one additional model size per family (e.g. T5-base and Pythia-410M or 2.8B) to show whether the robustness gain and the perplexity cost trend with scale.

## Reviewer 4 (senior generalist)

**Scores:** soundness 2, presentation 3, contribution 2, rating 4, confidence 4

### Summary

The paper constrains the feed-forward sublayers of pretrained Transformers to have elementwise nonnegative weights (via a softplus reparameterization initialized at |W_pre|), leaving attention, normalization, and residuals unconstrained, and fine-tunes the result. On T5-small (5 seeds, full CNN/DM and XSUM test sets) this costs 2–5% relative ROUGE-L and reduces HotFlip success from ~60% to 17% (single seed, n=200). On Pythia-1.4B (3 seeds) constraining both MLP projections reduces HotFlip success from 69% to 42.5% at a 5.6x perplexity cost; constraining only the input projection does not help. Transfer, random-substitution and gradient-free query controls on Pythia show the monotone model is less sensitive to every substitution type in relative terms, though absolute loss gaps are small. A conditional analysis (Section 5) argues that nonnegative Jacobians plus activation saturation attenuate the FFN branch's gradient contribution. A post-hoc probe diagnostic (Section 6.3) finds the constrained models are not more order-preserving along learned semantic directions, and on T5 are less so at intermediate depth. An ablation (App. A.10) attributes the clean-quality cost entirely to the sign-discarding initialization rather than the constraint; the corresponding robustness ablation is not run.

### Strengths

- Unusually honest reporting for an architectural-defense paper: the negative order-preservation result (Sec. 6.3, p. 8), the initialization ablation showing the clean-quality cost is not due to the constraint (App. A.10, pp. 24-25), absolute nat increases reported alongside relative degradation (Tables 2 and 4), the UAT truncation caveat, the GELU non-monotonicity caveat, and the non-budget-matched screening caveat are all stated plainly rather than buried.
- Good experimental hygiene on the T5 quality side: five training seeds, full test sets (11.5k / 11.3k examples), within-seed bootstrap CIs, per-example paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction, effect sizes, and across-seed tests (Sec. 6.1, App. A.5-A.6). Training curves are reported per epoch (Table 5).
- The Pythia track includes gradient-free (random substitution, query-based) and cross-model transfer controls (Table 4, p. 9; App. A.8-A.9), directly addressing the obfuscated-gradients concern (Athalye et al.) that most structural-defense papers ignore. The asymmetric transfer result is a genuinely interesting observation.
- The method is simple and practical: a softplus reparameterization applied to pretrained weights with no change to data, objective, or attack-time procedure (Sec. 3, p. 4), and the constraint-scope study (Table 7, App. A.9) yields a useful negative finding (constraining W_in alone increases attack success) that would save others effort.
- Protocol appendices (A.5-A.8) are detailed enough to re-implement: hyperparameters, seeds, decoding, candidate pools, thresholds, precision per stage, and hardware are all given.
- The convexity remark (Sec. 3, p. 4) that ReLU + nonnegative weights yields per-coordinate convex FFNs, and the explicit statement that with pre-LN the sublayer map F is not itself monotone in h (Remark A.2.2, p. 16), show the authors understand exactly what is and is not being enforced.

### Weaknesses

- Central attribution confound is unresolved and the resolving experiment is cheap. The monotone model differs from the baseline in two ways: the nonnegativity constraint and the sign-discarding |W_pre| initialization. App. A.10 (pp. 24-25) shows the initialization alone reproduces the entire clean-loss gap (abs-init-free: 2.51 vs monotone 2.51), yet the paper does not evaluate HotFlip on the abs-init-free or sign-frozen arms, explicitly leaving it to future work (p. 25; also flagged on p. 2 and p. 9). These two seed-42 models already exist; a 200-example HotFlip run on each would take minutes. Until this is done, the headline claim that *monotonicity* produces the robustness gain is not established: a poorly-initialized unconstrained model in a different basin could be equally 'robust' by the paper's metric.
- No clean-loss-matched control, and the success metric structurally favors the higher-loss model. The monotone T5 has clean loss 3.39 vs 2.77 nats (Sec. 6.2, p. 7); the monotone Pythia has 5.6x the perplexity (Sec. 6.4, p. 9). Success is defined as *relative* degradation above 10%/15%, so a model with higher clean loss needs a larger absolute perturbation to count as attacked. In absolute terms the Pythia HotFlip gap is 0.77 vs 0.82 nats (Table 4, p. 9), i.e. essentially none, and in Table 3 seed 2024 the monotone model is *worse* under UAT in absolute terms (0.56 vs 0.33 nats), which the main text does not discuss. The authors should add an unconstrained baseline degraded to matched clean loss (early checkpoint, weight noise, or reduced budget) and report success rates at an absolute-nat threshold.
- No comparison to any alternative structural constraint or defense. Section 2 and App. A.1 cite Lipschitz-bounded FFNs/attention (Kim et al., Newhouse et al., Nolte et al.), weight-norm control, and simple input-side baselines (Jain et al.), but none is run. Without at least one alternative constraint (e.g., spectral-norm-bounded or heavily weight-decayed FFN) at matched clean loss, the paper cannot show that monotonicity in particular, rather than any capacity-reducing constraint on the FFN, is responsible.
- The strongest robustness result rests on one seed and one attack, and the two tracks do not cover each other's gaps. The abstract's 60% -> 17% figure comes from Table 2 (p. 7): seed 2024 only, n=200, 5 flips, corroborated by a seed-42 run on n=100 (App. A.9, p. 24). Five seeds of checkpoints exist; HotFlip should be reported as mean +/- s.d. across all five, exactly as ROUGE is. Moreover, the gradient-free and transfer controls are run only on Pythia, where the absolute effect is marginal, while T5, where the effect is large, has no gradient-masking controls at all (Sec. 4, p. 4; Sec. 6.2, p. 7).
- The motivating 'semantic order' story (abstract; Sec. 1, pp. 1-2) is contradicted by the paper's own diagnostic and by what is actually enforced. The abstract states the models' 'semantic refinement is order-preserving'; but (i) all trained models use A = I, so 'semantic axes' are raw hidden coordinates (Sec. 3, p. 3); (ii) with pre-LN the sublayer F(h) = h + g(LN(h)) is not monotone in h at all, only the branch g in LN(h) (Remark A.2.2, p. 16); and (iii) Sec. 6.3 (p. 8, Fig. 1) shows the monotone T5 encoder drops to 0.42-0.45 order preservation at layers 3-5 vs 0.78-0.92 for the baseline, and monotone Pythia is worse at every depth. The general A-monotone construction (Def. 3.4, Prop. 3.3, p. 3) is never instantiated with p < d. The abstract, introduction, contribution list, and title should be rewritten to describe what was done: a nonnegative-weight FFN constraint whose measured effect is a reduction in local sensitivity, not order preservation.
- Section 5 (pp. 5-6) occupies a full page with elementary results that yield no tested prediction. Lemma 5.1 is 'a product of nonnegative matrices is nonnegative'; Lemma 5.3 is Cauchy-Schwarz. The paper concedes that 'Nonnegativity is not needed for the bound' (p. 6), that whether saturation actually occurs is 'an empirical question we do not test' (p. 6), and that J_T >= I so the constraint does not by itself reduce gradient magnitude. The obvious validation is missing: report the fraction of inactive ReLU units in constrained vs baseline FFNs on the attacked examples, and the embedding-gradient norm per model. Either add this diagnostic or compress Section 5 to a half page and use the space for the missing controls above.
- Threat-model mismatch between motivation and measurement. The abstract and Sec. 1 motivate the work with jailbreaks and unsafe outputs, but every robustness number is a teacher-forced loss increase (Sec. 4, p. 4; App. A.8). No task-level metric under HotFlip is reported (ROUGE under attack is given only for UATs, where the attack is ineffective), and no safety-relevant evaluation of any kind is included. At minimum, report ROUGE-L of generated summaries under HotFlip for the three T5 models.
- The T5 universal-trigger experiment is invalidated by the protocol: the trigger is prepended and then the input is left-truncated to 512 tokens, so the trigger is discarded for articles longer than ~505 tokens (Sec. 4, p. 4; App. A.8, pp. 21-22). The sub-1% NLL increases therefore carry no information. This is a one-line fix (truncate the article first, then prepend) and should be rerun rather than reported as a 'lower bound'.
- The causal-LM HotFlip is a weak attack and the loss convention inflates all Pythia figures. On the baseline, gradient-guided substitutions (55.2%) are *less* damaging than random ones (64.6%) (App. A.9, p. 24), which indicates the implementation's first-order search is worse than chance; and because substituted/inserted tokens are themselves prediction targets (App. A.8, p. 22), every reported degradation includes the low likelihood of the injected tokens. Score only unperturbed positions, and consider a stronger attacker (e.g., GCG-style multi-candidate search) so that the Pythia comparison is meaningful. The scope screening (Table 7) is additionally single-seed and not budget-matched, as acknowledged.
- Novelty is modest relative to the cited literature (Sec. 2, App. A.1). Nonnegative-weight monotone networks date to Sill (1998) and Daniels & Velikova (2010); Incer et al. (2018) already showed monotone classifiers resist evasion attacks; nonnegative MLP weights for interpretability appear in Chorowski & Zurada. The contribution is applying this to FFN sublayers of pretrained Transformers and measuring the effect, which is legitimate but does not justify 'We introduce monotonicity as an architectural inductive bias' (abstract).
- Reproducibility and setup issues: code and per-seed result files are promised only for the camera-ready (Reproducibility statement, p. 10), with no anonymized repository. The T5 fine-tuning corpus (DialogSum, HighlightSum, arXiv abstracts; App. A.5) is unusual and evaluation is OOD: the fine-tuned baseline does not beat the un-fine-tuned checkpoint on CNN/DM ROUGE-L (33.2 vs 33.2, Table 1) and XSUM ROUGE-L is 13, so the summarization setting is far from a realistic deployment. Pythia uses bfloat16 weights (App. A.7); the paper should state whether softplus(V) for large-negative V underflows to exactly zero in bf16, effectively pruning weights.
- Presentation: the paper reads as a chain of caveats, and the contribution list (p. 2, 'Substantial gains in adversarial robustness') and the phrase 'substantially greater robustness in both settings' (Sec. 1, p. 2) are not supported by the Pythia absolute figures. The main-text Section 5 and the near-verbatim App. A.4 duplicate each other (A.4 says it 'reproduces the complete text'), which wastes appendix space that the missing experiments should occupy.

### Questions

- Please run HotFlip (and if possible the random-substitution and query controls) on the seed-42 sign-frozen and abs-init-free arms from App. A.10. Do they show the same robustness as the monotone model? This single experiment determines whether the paper's central claim holds.
- What are the T5 HotFlip degradation and success rates across all five training seeds (mean +/- s.d.), rather than seed 2024 alone?
- On Pythia-1.4B, how does an unconstrained baseline with held-out perplexity matched to ~37 (e.g., an early or reduced-budget checkpoint) fare under the same HotFlip, random and query attacks? Similarly for T5 at matched clean loss (~3.4 nats).
- What are the success rates when the threshold is an absolute increase in nats (e.g., 0.2 nats) rather than a relative 10%/15%?
- Does the constrained FFN actually operate in the saturated regime Section 5 assumes? Please report the fraction of ReLU units with zero activation per layer for monotone vs baseline T5, and the norm of the loss gradient w.r.t. token embeddings on the attacked examples.
- What is ROUGE-L of the generated summaries under HotFlip for the standard, baseline and monotone T5 models? A 0.14-nat loss increase may or may not change outputs.
- Can the T5 UAT experiment be rerun with the article truncated before the trigger is prepended, so that the trigger is present for every example?
- For the Pythia loss convention, what are the degradations when only the unperturbed target positions are scored?
- With bfloat16 weights in the Pythia runs, does W = softplus(V) underflow to exactly zero for strongly negative V, and if so what fraction of constrained weights are exactly zero after recovery training?
- Have you tried any alternative capacity-reducing constraint on the same FFN matrices (spectral-norm bound, heavy weight decay, or the Lipschitz-monotone construction of Nolte et al./Kitouni et al. that you cite) to test whether nonnegativity specifically matters?

### Rating justification

This is a carefully executed and unusually honest empirical study of a simple idea, and I credit the multi-seed full-test-set quality evaluation, the gradient-free controls on Pythia, and the willingness to report the negative order-preservation result and the initialization ablation. But as submitted the paper cannot support its headline claim. The monotone model differs from the baseline in both the constraint and a sign-discarding initialization; the authors' own ablation shows the initialization alone explains the entire clean-loss gap, and they do not run the corresponding (trivially cheap) robustness evaluation on those ablation arms, so the robustness gain cannot be attributed to monotonicity. The success metric is relative to each model's own clean loss, which favors the worse-fitting model; there is no loss-matched or alternative-constraint baseline; and on Pythia the absolute gaps nearly vanish (0.77 vs 0.82 nats for HotFlip) while perplexity rises 5.6x. The single-seed T5 headline lacks the gradient-masking controls that the Pythia track has, and the Pythia track's effect is marginal. The semantic-order framing in the abstract and introduction is contradicted by Section 6.3 and by what is actually enforced (A = I, pre-LN). Section 5 is elementary and makes no tested prediction. Novelty over classical nonnegative-weight monotone networks and Incer et al. (2018) is modest. The gap between this submission and an acceptable paper is a small number of well-defined experiments (robustness of the ablation arms, a loss-matched control, HotFlip across all five seeds, a saturation diagnostic) plus a rewritten framing; without them it sits between reject and marginally below threshold, and I score it 4.

### What would raise the score

- Evaluate HotFlip and the gradient-free controls on the sign-frozen and abs-init-free ablation arms (App. A.10) and show the constrained model is more robust than both; this is the single most important missing experiment.
- Add an unconstrained baseline at matched clean loss / perplexity for both tracks, and report success rates at an absolute-nat threshold in addition to the relative one.
- Add at least one alternative FFN constraint (spectral-norm / Lipschitz bound or heavy weight decay) at matched clean loss to show that nonnegativity specifically, not any capacity reduction, drives the effect.
- Report T5 HotFlip across all five seeds with mean +/- s.d., and run the random-substitution and query controls on T5 as well.
- Empirically validate the Section 5 mechanism (fraction of saturated units, embedding-gradient norms) or shorten Section 5 substantially.
- Report a task-level robustness metric (ROUGE under HotFlip) and, ideally, one safety-relevant evaluation consistent with the jailbreak motivation.
- Fix the UAT truncation protocol and the Pythia loss convention (score only unperturbed positions), and use a stronger causal-LM attacker than the current HotFlip, which underperforms random substitution on the baseline.
- Rewrite the abstract, title, introduction and contribution list so that the claims match Section 6.3 and Remark A.2.2: a nonnegative-weight FFN constraint with a measured local-sensitivity effect, not order-preserving semantic refinement.
- Provide an anonymized code and per-seed results release at submission time.
